AUTOMATIC DATASET GENERATION - Perseverance rover in OmniLRS / Isaac Sim
One-slide brief: feed this to a slide generator
================================================================================

TITLE: The simulator labels its own data

ONE-LINE PITCH
    --dataset-out turns the simulator into a data generator: a seeded random
    operator drives the rover, faults appear on a seeded random schedule, and
    the whole run is recorded twice - once as the ground could see it, once as
    the simulator knows it. The injected faults ARE the labels.

    Command: run_perseverance.py --headless --dataset-out DIR --episodes 100
    No ground station involved.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PIPELINE - four modules, one loop
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    seed --> FaultScheduler   : samples ONE fault schedule per episode, up front
         --> MissionScripter  : the operator, replaced by an RNG
         --> EpisodeRunner    : resets, steps physics, plays the schedule
         --> DatasetRecorder  : writes observable/ and oracle/ per episode

  BLOCK DIAGRAM

   dataset_seed
        |
        v
   episode_seed = seed*100003 + index      (one seed -> everything)
        |
        +---> FaultScheduler.generate() -----> schedule.json  (sampled up front)
        +---> MissionScripter(seed)      \
        +---> random.seed()  (noise models)\
        |                                   v
        v                          +-------------------------------+
   _reset_episode()                |   PER-STEP LOOP (~33 ms)      |
   - clear all faults              |  1. pop due fault events      |
   - teleport + random heading     |     -> injector.inject_*()    |
   - battery -> 100%               |  2. scripter issues next cmd  |
   - face temps -> initial         |  3. drive_controller.update() |
   - OBC uptime restart            |  4. fault_injector.update()   |
   - GO/NOGO -> GO                 |  5. power + thermal integrate |
   - randomize rocks (/terrain)    |  6. downlink tick   @ 1 Hz    |
   - stamp/remove crater (--crater)|  7. nav-cam frame   @ 0.2 Hz  |
   - settle 30 steps               |                               |
                                   +-------------------------------+
                                                |
                                                v
                                   observable/  |  oracle/
                                   telemetry.csv|  truth.csv
                                   commands.json|  faults.jsonl
                                   images/      |  schedule.json

  Loop ORDER is load-bearing and identical to the interactive loop: commands,
  drive, faults, power - all between world.step() calls. Nothing writes USD off
  the simulation thread.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IDEA 1 - THE SCHEDULE IS SAMPLED, NOT SCRIPTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Episode class          share   what happens
  --------------------   -----   -----------------------------------------
  nominal                 60%    no fault at any point
  single                  28%    one fault (25% of those recover on their own)
  concurrent               9%    two different kinds overlapping in time
  sequential               3%    one recovers, then another begins
  crater (--crater only)  +0.15  steep crater; trapped 70% / avoid 15% /
                                 skirt 15%, 30% also carry one fault

  THREE SAMPLING RULES, each from what the dataset is FOR:

  MAJORITY NOMINAL     a detector needs negatives -> 60% of episodes clean

  ONSET IN THE MIDDLE  every onset lands in the 40-60% window, never t=0, so
                       each faulted episode holds THREE PHASES:
                       clean baseline -> transition -> settled faulted regime.
                       A fault at t=0 teaches regime recognition, not detection.

  SEVERITY >= 0.30     a fault too weak to have an effect is a MISLABELLED
                       nominal episode - worse than no episode at all.

  Consequence worth stating: episode-level 40% faulted, but SAMPLE-level only
  ~15-20% of timesteps. The second number is what training actually sees, so the
  manifest reports both.

  Windows are FRACTIONS of the episode, so changing episode length rescales the
  phases proportionally. All tunable under dataset.faults in the robot config.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IDEA 2 - THE OPERATOR IS A SEEDED RNG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  A fault's signature depends on what the rover was in the middle of. So the
  mission is randomized too: goto (60%) / drive_straight (25%) / drive_turn
  (15%), chained ONE AT A TIME, the next issued when the last completes.

  Completion is decided on MEASURED POSE, not elapsed time -> a weakened wheel
  that makes a leg take twice as long simply makes it take twice as long,
  instead of the mission moving on without it.

  Waypoints drawn inside an 8 m disc around the episode start, clamped to the
  terrain, min 2.5 m per leg. Commands pass through the SAME tc_loss gate the
  real uplink uses, so a comms fault is visible even with no ground station.

  Rare HOUSEKEEPING between legs (~1 per episode): power a device off
  (motor controller or camera) or stow the solar panel, then undo it after a
  sampled hold - so a nominal episode also contains legitimate off-nominal
  commands that a detector must not flag.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IDEA 3 - OBSERVABLE vs ORACLE (the point of the whole exercise)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  A detector may only be given what a real mission could see.

  observable/   the downlink, and NOT a re-derivation of it - the recorder
                drives the SAME PerseveranceTransmitter the ground station
                drives, pointed at a file. 42 of the 58 downlinked
                parameters (pose_ground_truth and the 15 /Rover/faults
                parameters go to oracle); a parameter added to the rover
                appears in the dataset with no code change.

  oracle/       what no rover could know: the clean pre-corruption IMU reading
                AND THE EXACT RESIDUAL, true battery Wh ahead of the
                measurement noise, the real torque limit on every joint,
                each wheel's damping factor, friction and sinkage, crater
                geometry and signed rim distance (with --crater),
                ground-truth health of every subsystem, and the label.

  THE LABEL comes from the INJECTOR, not from the schedule: the schedule says
  what was meant to happen, the injector says what did. A fault set another way
  (startup flag, live operator) is still labelled correctly.

  Three judgement calls are TAGGED rather than hidden:
      pose_ground_truth.*        -> moved to oracle (it is the answer)
      command_distance/heading   -> observable, tagged derived_from_truth
      contact_force_*            -> observable, tagged derived_from_truth
  manifest.json -> columns.notes lists them; drop for strict observability.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IDEA 4 - A COMMS FAULT IS MISSING DATA, NOT A FLAG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  tm_loss is drawn PER PARAMETER, so each column gets its own independent gaps
  and a lost value is a genuinely EMPTY CELL (pandas NaN), never smoothed over.
  tc_loss drops the command before the rover sees it, while the ground's log
  still records it as sent - that DISCREPANCY is the observable fault.

  Columns are registered BEFORE the drop gate. Otherwise a parameter dropped on
  every tick of a heavily faulted episode would have no column at all and that
  episode could not be concatenated with the others.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IDEA 5 - UNATTENDED MEANS HANDLING THE BAD CASES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  RESET IS THE HARD PART. Nothing in the subsystems stack had a reset: battery
  charge, face temperatures, OBC uptime, GO/NOGO and every injected fault
  persist for the life of the process. An episode inheriting the previous one's
  flat battery would be LABELLED NOMINAL and look nothing like one.

  FALLING OFF THE TERRAIN (seen for real: 13 of 20 episodes in run01). A
  drive_straight goes wherever the rover faces, and waypoint clamping never
  limited it, so the rover drove over the edge and free-fell ~25 s until the
  500 m sanity check fired - labelled "physics diverged", with the fall still
  in the rows. Now every straight is cut short where its heading leaves the
  bounds (or becomes a goto), and the runner checks the pose against the height
  map each step: outside the DEM or 0.5 m below the ground abandons the episode
  as "aborted: fell off terrain" and discards the last 3 s (tipping over the
  edge) - rows, commands, fault events and frames. meta.json records the cut.

  SOLVER DIVERGENCE. A non-finite pose or one beyond 500 m still abandons the
  episode as "aborted: physics diverged", KEEPS the rows already recorded, and
  gives the next reset extra settle steps. If the rover never comes back, the
  run STOPS and says so.

  RE-RUN SAFETY. Each episode directory is cleared as it is written, or a
  shorter re-run leaves the previous run's frames sitting in images/, indexed
  nowhere and looking exactly like this episode's data. Consumers iterate
  manifest.json -> episode_index, never the directory listing.

  DISK GUARD. Refuses to start an episode below --min-free-gb, stops cleanly at
  --max-disk-gb. ~20 MB/episode with frames, ~2 GB per 100 episodes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NUMBERS FOR THE SLIDE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    episode            600 s = 18000 physics steps @ 33 ms
    telemetry          1 Hz, 42 observable parameters -> ~600 rows/episode
    nav-cam            0.2 Hz, 320x240 RGBA -> ~120 frames/episode
    fault kinds        10 (wheel torque/stuck/slip/sink, steer torque/stuck,
                           imu, camera, battery, comms)
                       + crater episodes with --crater (not a fault: the rover
                         is intact but trapped)
    class mix          60 / 28 / 9 / 3 %  (nominal/single/concurrent/sequential)
    faulted timesteps  ~15-20%
    disk               ~20 MB/episode, ~2 GB per 100
    format             CSV + JSONL (no pyarrow/h5py in the Isaac Sim python)

    REPRODUCIBILITY, honestly stated: one --dataset-seed fixes every episode
    seed, schedule and waypoint. PhysX determinism is NOT configured, so
    episodes replay IN DISTRIBUTION rather than bit-exactly - and the manifest
    says so rather than leaving it to be discovered.
